## Part 1 — Why Google Can't Find You (Yet)

### The core problem: Google doesn't know where you work

Think of Google as the world's largest directory. For it to recommend you when someone types "massage therapist Bristol", it needs three things:
1. To know you're a massage therapist
2. To know you serve Bristol (or work within it)
3. To believe you're worth recommending (your credibility signals)

If any of these three is missing, Google won't show you — regardless of how good you are.

### The reality for 90% of local freelancers

Most independent professionals I speak to fall into one of these situations:

**Situation A: No website**
Google can't reference what it can't read. Without a website, you only exist to people who already know you.

**Situation B: A website with no local information**
Your site mentions your name and services, but not the areas you cover. Google sees you as a professional "somewhere in the UK" — not as "the physio in Cheltenham".

**Situation C: Only a Google Business Profile**
Better than nothing, but Google Business Profile alone isn't enough for searches like "mobile personal trainer [neighbourhood]". It's too limited for freelancers covering multiple areas.

**Situation D: Relying on a third-party platform (Bark, Treatwell, Checkatrade...)**
These platforms do their own SEO. You depend on their algorithm, not yours. You pay for visibility you don't own — and they can change the rules at any time.

## Part 2 — How Local Google Search Really Works

### The 3 types of local results

When someone searches "electrician Manchester", Google shows three distinct result types:

**1. The Local Pack (the 3 Google Maps listings)**
These appear first, with a map. To appear here, you need a complete Google Business Profile with your service area defined.

**2. Organic results**
These appear below the Local Pack. To appear here, you need a website optimised for your town or service area.

**3. Directories and platforms**
Yell, Checkatrade, Bark, Treatwell occupy many of these slots. You can appear on them for free, but you have no control.

**The winning strategy: be present in all three.**

### Why local search intent is the most valuable traffic on the internet

"emergency plumber Birmingham" = someone with a burst pipe right now.
"physio home visits Leeds" = someone who needs treatment at home.
"wedding photographer Edinburgh" = someone planning their big day.

These searches share one thing: **95% purchase intent**. These aren't casual browsers. They're people ready to call or book.

This is why local SEO is the highest-return acquisition channel for freelancers — far more cost-effective than Facebook ads, leaflets, or passive word-of-mouth.

## Part 3 — The 5 Levers to Rank First Locally

### Lever 1 — A website with the right local signals

Your website is the foundation of everything. Without it, levers 2–5 work at half power.

**What your website must include:**

*Your town and service areas, stated clearly*

Don't write "serving the Greater Manchester area". Write:
"I'm a self-employed physiotherapist based in Didsbury. I provide home visits across Manchester, Stockport, Cheadle, and Sale."

Google reads these place names and understands you're relevant for searches in those areas.

*Your services written with the right keywords*

Instead of "Home visits available", write "Physiotherapy home visits in Manchester and surrounding areas — south Manchester, Stockport, and Cheadle". You're naturally incorporating the terms your future clients are searching.

*Your contact details on every page (or at minimum in the footer)*

Name, phone, email, and critically: your town and service area. Google cross-references this to confirm your local anchor.

**Practical tip:** create a separate page for each significant area you cover. If you're a plumber serving Manchester, Salford, and Trafford, create:
- A main page for Manchester
- A dedicated page or section for "plumber Salford"
- Likewise for Trafford

This isn't manipulation — it's making relevant information accessible.

---

### Lever 2 — Your Google Business Profile

This is non-negotiable. Free. And massively underused.

**How to optimise your profile in 30 minutes:**

**Step 1 — Verify or create your listing**
Go to business.google.com. If your profile doesn't exist, create it. If it exists but isn't claimed, claim it.

**Step 2 — Fill in everything**
- Primary category (be specific: "Physiotherapist", not "Healthcare professional")
- Description (200 words, naturally including your service towns)
- Opening hours
- Photos (at least 5: your workspace, you working, your equipment, your results)
- Service area: list each town individually

**Step 3 — Service areas: the field everyone forgets**
In your Google Business Profile, you can define a "service area" instead of (or in addition to) a fixed address. This is critical for mobile and home-visit professionals. Add every town or postcode district where you work.

**Step 4 — Reviews: your most powerful ranking signal**
Profiles with the most positive reviews appear first in the Local Pack. After every completed job or session, ask your satisfied client to leave a Google review. Reply to every review — this signals to Google that you're an active, engaged business.

---

### Lever 3 — Schema.org Structured Data (the expert's edge)

This one is less well known but quietly very effective.

Google doesn't read your website the way a human does. It parses your HTML to understand who you are and where you operate. Structured data is a standardised "language" you add to your site to tell Google explicitly:

"I am a physiotherapist. My name is Sarah Jones. I serve Manchester, Stockport, and Cheadle. Here is my phone number. Here are my hours."

The code looks like this (you don't need to write it yourself — the right platforms generate it automatically):

```json
{
  "@type": "LocalBusiness",
  "name": "Sarah Jones Physiotherapy",
  "areaServed": ["Manchester", "Stockport", "Cheadle"],
  "telephone": "+44 7XXX XXXXXX"
}
```

With this data in place, Google can display your phone number directly in search results — without the user needing to click through to your site.

### Lever 4 — Local Directories and Citations

A "citation" in SEO terms is a mention of your business name, address, and phone number on a third-party site.

The more consistent citations you have on trusted sites, the more Google validates your local existence.

**Key directories to complete (free):**

| Directory | Time required | SEO impact |
|-----------|--------------|------------|
| Google Business Profile | 30 min | Critical |
| Yell.com | 15 min | High |
| Yelp | 15 min | High |
| Checkatrade (trades) | 20 min | High |
| Bark.com | 15 min | High |
| FreeIndex | 10 min | Medium |
| Thomson Local | 10 min | Medium |
| Your professional body / register | Variable | Very high |

**Golden rule:** your business name, address, and phone number must be **identical** across all directories. Even small variations (Street vs St / 07123 vs 07123 456 789) weaken your local authority.

### Lever 5 — Local Content (the difference between page 1 and position 1)

The first four levers get you onto page one. This fifth lever gets you to the top.

**The idea:** create genuinely useful, locally-anchored content on your website.

For a personal trainer in Bristol, this could be:
- "The 5 best parks in Bristol for outdoor training sessions"
- "Home personal training in Clifton — what to expect from your first session"
- "Fitness in Bedminster: why I started offering home visits in south Bristol"

This doesn't need to be long or technically complex. It needs to be useful, locally relevant, and consistent with your services.

Google values this content because it is:
- Useful (answers a real question)
- Local (mentions specific places)
- Fresh (updated regularly)
- Coherent (matches your services)

**Recommended frequency:** one article or content page per month. Quality over quantity — a genuinely useful 600-word article beats a thin 2,000-word one.

## Part 5 — The 3 Mistakes That Kill Local Visibility

### Mistake 1 — Depending entirely on a third-party platform

Checkatrade, Bark, Treatwell, Doctify — these platforms are intermediaries. They can change their algorithms, raise their fees, or drown you in local competition. Your digital presence cannot rest on a platform you don't control.

Your website is your property. Your Google Business Profile is your property. These must be your foundation.

### Mistake 2 — Not specifying your service areas

This is the single most common and costly omission. You might cover five towns, but if your website doesn't mention them, Google assumes you only serve your primary address.

Name every town and area you serve — in your page text, not just your contact details.

### Mistake 3 — Building a website and never touching it again

A site that hasn't been updated in two years looks abandoned to Google. Add content regularly — even a short update about new availability, a new service, or a brief blog post about a local event relevant to your work.
